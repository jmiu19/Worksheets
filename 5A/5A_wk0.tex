\noindent \textit{Example}: If the force $F$ acting on an object of mass $m$ (measured in kilograms) and acceleration $a$ (measured in meters per second squared) obeys the formula $F = ma$, then the unit of the force is
\begin{align*}
[F] = [m]\cdot [a] = \text{(kilogram)} \cdot \text{(meters per second squared)} = \text{kg\,m\,/\,s}^2\,.
\end{align*}
Later on we will call this new unit (kg\,m/s$^2$) as Newton (N).  The letter $m$ is italicized when we use it to denote mass, but m is not italicized when we use it to denote the unit meter.\\

\section*{Problem 1}
I recommend first converting everything to the desired unit and then doing the algebra and computations, so we first write
\begin{align*}
r = 5 \text{ m} = \frac{5}{0.305}\text{ ft}\,,
\end{align*}
then we calculate the area
\begin{align*}
A = \pi r^2 = \pi \cdot \left( \frac{5}{0.305} \text{ ft}\right)^2= \pi \cdot \left( \frac{5}{0.305} \right)^2 \text{ ft}^2 \approx 844 \ \text{ft}^2\,.
\end{align*}
Make sure you have the correct unit in your final answer. For this class, when it comes to decimal places, I recommend keeping $3$ or $4$ significant digits unless specified otherwise.\\

\section*{Problem 2}
We want to find the unit of $B$, and the units of all other quantities are known, so we rearrange the $F = \rho v^2 B$ formula to isolate $B$, 
\begin{align*}
B = \frac{F}{\rho v^2}\,,
\end{align*}
then we just plug in the units and get
\begin{align*}
[B] = \frac{\text{kg m/s}^2}{(\text{kg/m}^3)\cdot (\text{m/s})^2} = \text{m}^2\,. 
\end{align*}
Now we see that $B$ has the unit of m$^2$, which is something like an ``Area". \\

\section*{Problem 3}
The angle at Michael is a right angle, so the distances from Jinyan to Michael and from Michael to Albert are the two sides of a right triangle. The distance from Jinyan to Albert is the hypotenuse. Using the Pythagorean theorem, we have
\begin{align*}
(\text{Jinyan--Michael})^2+(\text{Michael--Albert})^2=(\text{Jinyan--Albert})^2\,.
\end{align*}
Then we plug in the two known distances and get
\begin{align*}
(4\,\text{m})^2+(3\,\text{m})^2&=(\text{Jinyan--Albert})^2\,,\\
\text{Jinyan--Albert}&=\sqrt{(4\,\text{m})^2+(3\,\text{m})^2}\\
&=\sqrt{16+9}\,\text{m}=5\,\text{m}\,.
\end{align*}
To find the angle $\theta$ at Jinyan, the $3$ m side is opposite to $\theta$ and the $4$ m side is adjacent to $\theta$, so we use the tangent function,
\begin{align*}
\tan(\theta)=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}=\frac{3\,\text{m}}{4\,\text{m}}=\frac{3}{4}\,.
\end{align*}
Taking the inverse tangent of both sides gives the angle in degrees,
\begin{align*}
\theta=\arctan\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)=36.9^\circ\,.
\end{align*}
Finally, we convert this angle from degrees to radians,
\begin{align*}
\theta=36.9^\circ\cdot\frac{\pi\,\text{rad}}{180^\circ}=0.644\,\text{rad}\,.
\end{align*}
Thus the distance between Jinyan and Albert is $5$ m, and the angle is $36.9^\circ$, or equivalently $0.644$ rad.\\

\section*{Problem 4}
The line segment $BA$ is vertical, so it makes an angle $90^\circ$ with the horizontal. The line segment $AC$ is parallel to the incline and makes an angle $30^\circ$ with the horizontal. Therefore, the angle at $A$ in the small right triangle is
\begin{align*}
\angle BAC=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ\,.
\end{align*}
Since $\angle ACB=90^\circ$, the three angles of the triangle give
\begin{align*}
\angle ABC=180^\circ-\angle BAC-\angle ACB=180^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ=30^\circ\,.
\end{align*}
The line segment $BA$ is the hypotenuse with a ``length" of $100$ N. Relative to $\angle ABC=30^\circ$, the line segment $AC$ is the opposite side, so
\begin{align*}
\sin(30^\circ)=\frac{AC}{BA}=\frac{AC}{100\,\text{N}}\,.
\end{align*}
We thus find
\begin{align*}
AC=(100\,\text{N})\sin(30^\circ)=(100\,\text{N})\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=50.0\,\text{N}\,.
\end{align*}
The line segment $BC$ is the adjacent side, so
\begin{align*}
\cos(30^\circ)=\frac{BC}{BA}=\frac{BC}{100\,\text{N}}\,.
\end{align*}
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
BC=(100\,\text{N})\cos(30^\circ)=(100\,\text{N})\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)=86.6\,\text{N}\,.
\end{align*}
Thus the force component parallel to the incline has a ``length" $AC=50.0$ N, while the force component perpendicular to the incline has a ``length" $BC=86.6$ N.\\
